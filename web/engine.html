How the model thinks

No crystal ball — just statistics. For every match the engine works out the chance of a home win, a draw, or an away win, then checks whether the bookmaker’s price is generous enough to bet. Here’s the whole pipeline, with a picture for each idea.

STEP 1 · TEAM STRENGTH — Elo & Glicko-2

Rate every team

Every side has a strength rating. Beat a strong team and it climbs; lose and it dips — that’s the classic Elo idea. Glicko-2 adds a second number: how sure we are. A team that plays often has a tight band; a rusty or unknown side has a wide one.

The dot is the rating; the band is our uncertainty. Results slide the dot along the scale; playing regularly shrinks the band.

References — Elo, A. E. (1978). The Rating of Chessplayers, Past and Present. Arco Pub.
Glickman, M. E. (2012). Example of the Glicko-2 System. Boston University, pp. 1–6.

🔥
STEP 2 · MOMENTUM

Catch who’s hot

We also track the surprise in recent results — a team winning games it “shouldn’t”. That form signal flags a side riding a wave before its rating fully catches up.

📰
STEP 3 · THE NEWS — semantic analysis

Read the headlines

An AI scans recent, reputable coverage and pulls out what matters — key injuries, a settled or sacked coach, morale, a player in red-hot form. Semantic analysis means it judges by meaning, not keywords, and only for the players on that team. The verdict nudges the rating.

🎯
STEP 4 · THE FORECAST — Dixon-Coles

Turn strength into a scoreline

From the two strengths the engine estimates how many goals each side should score, then works out the chance of every scoreline. Add up the green squares for a home win, the diagonal for a draw, the blue for an away win — three numbers that always total 100%.

Every scoreline gets a probability; summing the three zones gives Home / Draw / Away.

Reference — Dixon, M. J. & Coles, S. G. (1997). Modelling association football scores and inefficiencies in the football betting market. Journal of the Royal Statistical Society: Series C (Applied Statistics), 46(2), 265–280.

✅
STEP 5 · HONESTY CHECK — calibration

Make the percentages mean what they say

We replay past matches and bend the model’s confidence until “predicted” lines up with “what really happened”. After the fix, a 70% really means about 70%.

The dashed line is “perfect”. The red curve is over-confident; calibration pulls it onto the line.

Reference — Guo, C., Pleiss, G., Sun, Y. & Weinberger, K. Q. (2017). On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks. Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML). arXiv:1706.04599.

💰
STEP 6 · FINDING VALUE

Compare to the price

A good bet isn’t the most likely result — it’s one the odds under-rate. The model backs an outcome only when its own % clearly beats the bookmaker’s implied %, so it won’t chase long-shots on noise.

Model says (Home)62%
Odds imply (Home)55%
→ a 7-point edge — worth backing ✅

Six players bet it out

STEP 8 · PROOF

Test it on the past

Before trusting it, we replay whole past tournaments as if they hadn’t happened yet. The model beats a no-skill baseline every time — real predictive skill, not luck. (backtesting)
